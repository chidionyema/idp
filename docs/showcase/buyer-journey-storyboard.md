# Buyer journey storyboard

The buyer's first contact with the platform, told as a stage-by-stage
story a buyer's engineer can re-enact. The artefacts referenced below
are real, on disk, and graded.

## Stage 1 — the 30-second hook

The buyer opens `/showcase` on the estate's portal. They see three
things before anything is clicked:

- The estate bar — `ELITE / GAP / BLIND` counts and the standards
  table live from `docs/SHOWCASE.md` (regenerated by
  `bin/estate-showcase`). The numbers are real, the page is fresh.
- The five Otto capabilities marked LIVE in
  `docs/specs/otto-capability-inventory.md`, each with a one-line
  proof file reference.
- The **Launch the buyer sandbox** button.

The buyer concludes: "the platform audits itself; the button proves I
can use it without asking."

## Stage 2 — the 5-minute self-prove

The buyer clicks *Launch the buyer sandbox*, picks `1h`. The press
runs the same `flux create kustomization demo-sandbox` command the
runbook already documents. Within two minutes:

- `flux get sources git -n flux-system sandbox-launch` reports
  `Ready True`.
- `flux get kustomizations -n flux-system demo-sandbox` reports
  `Ready True`.
- `kubectl -n demo-sandbox get pods` shows `demo-sandbox-0 Running`.

The shop answers at `https://sandbox.<zone>` through the shared edge.
The buyer deploys, lists, breaks, and restores anything they like —
it is bounded, catalogued, mortal.

The buyer concludes: "self-service is real; it does not need me to
ping ops; it expires without me."

## Stage 3 — the 30-minute deep dive

The buyer stays for the countdown. The Kustomization's
`cleanup.kyverno.io/ttl` label and creation timestamp are read through
the same Kubernetes proxy `useEstate.ts` already uses; the bar
shrinks in real time, the line below reads "N minutes left, then it
is gone".

The buyer opens `bin/estate-showcase --check` — it exits `0`. The
buyer opens `bin/idp-ci` — every gate runs. The buyer opens
`tests/test_demo_sandbox_is_defined_and_expires.py` — the open-source
image, the quarter-core ceiling, the no-storage rule, the standing
area and front door, the button choices, and the sweep are pinned.

The buyer asks Investigate (`/investigate` → `/holmes` proxy): "what
is running inside demo-sandbox right now?" The answer is a structured
read of the namespace.

The buyer concludes: "the SLO is a number, not a slide; the artefacts
are in the repo, not on the website."

## Why this storyboard is one page and not a deck

A buyer's engineer reads the runbook, not the slide deck. The
30-second hook, the 5-minute self-prove, and the 30-minute deep dive
are three clicks on the portal and three commands on the terminal.
This page names them. The screenshots, screencasts, and screenshots
go in the runbook itself
([`docs/runbooks/demo-sandbox.md`](../runbooks/demo-sandbox.md)).

The storyboard stays this short on purpose: the assets a buyer
actually uses are the artefacts on disk.
